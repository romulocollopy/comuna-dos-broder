# Securities Opinion — what it is and why it matters

Plain-language reference (not legal advice). The first 🔴 item in the contract's
"Pontos para o advogado".

## The concept

In Brazil, **valores mobiliários** (securities) are, broadly, arrangements where people
put in money expecting a return produced by **someone else's effort**. The classic test
(from US law, adopted in Brazilian doctrine/CVM and in Canada) is the **"investment
contract"**: (1) money invested, (2) in a common enterprise, (3) with an **expectation of
profit**, (4) from the **efforts of others**.

Brazil's catch-all: **Lei 6.385/76, art. 2º, IX — "contrato de investimento coletivo"** —
deliberately broad. The regulator is the **CVM** (Comissão de Valores Mobiliários).

## Why this project could *look* like a security

"Ten people pool money → buy land → hope it appreciates → share the gain." Squint and
that's a pooled investment. CVM — or a disgruntled ex-member — could argue it's an
unregistered collective investment.

## Why it is probably *not* one (the defenses)

1. **No expectation of profit** — purpose is shared living; rental income is *reinvested*,
   never distributed. A security, by definition, is about getting a return.
2. **Members are active co-owners, not passive investors** — everyone votes the big
   decisions; no one hands money to a "manager" to work it.
3. **Private and relationship-based** — friends, not a public offering to strangers.
4. **The contract says it** — recitals + Clauses 2 and 18 state the shared-living purpose
   and the "no return" understanding explicitly.

## What the "opinion" is

A written assessment from a Brazilian lawyer with capital-markets (CVM) experience who
reads the actual structure and opines: *"this is / isn't a security; this does / doesn't
require registration."* It is **not** a guarantee — it is a documented professional
judgment that gives the group a legal basis if the structure is ever challenged.

## What goes wrong without it

If the structure were found to be an unregistered collective investment:
- CVM sanctions and fines;
- the contract could be attacked as unenforceable;
- worst case, criminal exposure (offering securities without authorization);
- practically: a withdrawing member could argue "you sold me an illegal security — return
  everything."

## When to get it

**Before anyone transfers money.** It is the single most important piece of legal work
for the project.
