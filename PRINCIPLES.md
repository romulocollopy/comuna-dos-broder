# Principles

These are the design principles the whole initiative is built on. Every rule in the model and every clause in the eventual contract should be traceable back to one of these. They double as the "constitution" you present to the group — people should be able to read these and know whether they want in.

---

## 1. Voluntary, informed opt-in

Nobody is pressured in, and nobody is trapped. Joining is a free, informed decision; leaving is always possible, with consequences known *in advance* — never invented at exit time.

*Shows up as:* clear withdrawal rules defined before anyone contributes; a cooling-off/reading period before signing.

## 2. Transparency by default

Full visibility of the fund: balances, valuation, decisions, minutes, withdrawals. No hidden accounting. The members own the process, not just the asset.

*Shows up as:* periodic statements; open books; documented votes; a place where the rules and any changes to them live.

## 3. Proportionality with safeguards

Contribution ↔ ownership ↔ decision weight are proportional — but with democratic guardrails so a single large contributor can't unilaterally overrule the group on *communal* matters (like "do we sell the land").

*Shows up as:* capital-weighted votes for financial matters, per-person votes (or supermajorities) for communal matters.

## 4. Fair, predictable exit

Withdrawal is priced by rules agreed up front, not by negotiation in the moment. Early exit costs more than late exit; late exit should not punish the group.

*Shows up as:* a withdrawal valuation method + a penalty/vesting schedule known at signing.

## 5. Self-insurance, not speculation

The insurance reserve is a *safety mechanism*, not a profit center. It exists to make exits possible without forcing the group to sell land at a bad moment. If it's never needed, it flows back to the members.

*Shows up as:* the two-reserve structure; a defined "success" moment when the reserve unwinds.

## 6. Shared purpose over shared returns

The primary purpose is a shared place to live, not maximizing return. This is both an ethical choice *and* a legal shield: a genuine co-living/co-ownership arrangement is less likely to be re-characterized as an unregistered investment scheme.

*Shows up as:* the purpose clause of the contract; the governance emphasis on place, not profit.

## 7. Subsidiarity

Decisions happen at the lowest sensible level. Day-to-day administration is delegated; only material decisions (buying land, selling, admitting/removing members, changing rules) go to a group vote.

*Shows up as:* a decision matrix distinguishing reserved matters (group vote) from administrative matters (delegated).

## 8. Fiscal honesty

Structure to be *tax-correct and avoid double taxation*, never to evade. Cross-border money is reported on both sides where required. A clean tax position is part of what makes the whole thing durable.

*Shows up as:* the Brazil–Canada tax treaty awareness (Decreto 92.318/86); honest treatment of "money-equivalent" contributions as taxable barter.

## 9. Written-down, changeable-by-consent

Everything is written. Rules can change, but only through a defined process — not by surprise.

*Shows up as:* an amendment clause; versioned documents.

## 10. The land's income is collective capital, not profit

Any rent the land earns (from third parties, or from members before they move in) is **reinvested into the fund** — never distributed back to participants as income. Its only jobs are to grow the land-buying capacity (letting members stop paying earlier) and to cushion members in temporary hardship (discounts / postponements of payments).

*Shows up as:* a "reinvested income" clause; rental income routed to the land reserve; a hardship-postponement mechanism. This also reinforces principle 6 (shared purpose over returns) — and the securities posture, since no profit flows to members.

---

*These nine principles are the backbone's constitution. The branches (BR/CA) implement them in local legal form.*
