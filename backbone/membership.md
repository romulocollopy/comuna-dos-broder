# Backbone — Membership (family units & severability)

**Decision (2026-11-09):** membership is per-*family* in spirit, per-*individual* in law. A "family" is not a legal person, so the contract needs real parties.

## The rule

- **Legal members = individual natural persons** (each with CPF/passport, a real party to the contract).
- **A "family unit" = the named individuals of one household** (typically two spouses/partners) who **jointly hold one membership share**.
- The share is **expressly severable**: the contract pre-agrees what happens to it if the family splits. No surprises, no freezing-out.

## Why individuals, not "the family"

1. A family has no legal identity — it can't sign, hold a quota, or vote.
2. Individuals as members → clean ownership, clean voting, clean inheritance, clean tax.
3. Family protection does **not** come from making "family" a party; it comes from (a) naming **both** spouses as parties, (b) holding the share **jointly**, and (c) **pre-writing** the severance rules.

## The family share

- One family = one **share** (the standard participation unit). A share has a target quantum (e.g. "1 full share"); contributions feed the reserves and build the share's value.
- While joint, the family votes as **one** (single delegate). Neither spouse may unilaterally withdraw, transfer, or encumber the whole share.
- The two holders are **co-holders of the share**, and the share is flagged as matrimonial property in the contract (so it's part of the couple's estate, handled deliberately, not by default law alone).

## Divorce / separation — the four pre-agreed outcomes

On separation of a family unit's co-holders, the share is resolved **one of four ways**, chosen by the parties (or by a defined default if they can't agree):

| # | Outcome | What happens | Who becomes what |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Withdraw individually** | The departing holder exits their *portion* through the standard withdrawal mechanics (valuation × their sub-share) | One stays (reduced share); one exits with payout |
| 2 | **Compensate (buyout)** | One co-holder buys the other's portion at an agreed/valued price | One stays with the full share; the other is paid out |
| 3 | **Split in two** | The share is divided into **two independent half-shares** | Both stay, each as an **independent member** (independent vote + withdrawal rights) |
| 4 | **Top up to full** | After a split (outcome 3), a half-share holder adds capital to restore a **full share** | Both (or one) hold full shares again |

- **Outcome 3 (split) is the key protection:** it means divorce never forces anyone *out* of the community; it can instead create two independent, smaller memberships. This is what "split their share in 2 and have smaller unities" means, made mechanical.
- **Outcome 4 (top-up)** is the path from "smaller unit" back to "full unit," by adding money — an ordinary additional contribution by an existing member (no new admission vote), which increases their stake.
- **Default rule if the ex-partners can't agree:** outcome 3 (split) — it preserves both parties' place and avoids forcing a sale. (Confirm this default with the lawyer; some groups prefer a forced buyout.)

## Mechanics to lock down with the lawyer

- **Fractional participation:** splitting a quota into two half-quotas must be allowed by the vehicle (Brazilian Ltda quotas, cooperative "cotas-partes", or a partnership interest). Confirm fractional units are permitted, or define the share as 2 sub-units from the start.
- **Tax neutrality of the split:** a within-vehicle division of a share on divorce is generally not a taxable event, but confirm (divorce transfers may enjoy specific treatment).
- **Voting after split:** each half-share = one independent vote. This slightly changes the group's voting math — fine, just be aware.
- **Death (vs divorce):** on the death of one co-holder, the survivor's half is preserved (not auto-sold); the deceased's half follows inheritance + the buy-sell clause (§10).

## Why this is "easier legally" *and* protective

- **Easier:** no invented "family" legal entity; individuals sign, hold, vote, inherit — everything standard.
- **Protective:** both spouses are named parties, the share is joint and severable, and the four outcomes (especially split + top-up) guarantee neither spouse can be locked in or locked out.

## Non-owner participants (tenants / surface-holders)

Not everyone who joins the community needs to *own*. If a non-Brazilian national wants to live there on **rural** land (where Lei 5.709/71 restricts foreign ownership), they can join as a **non-owner participant**:

- **Tenant (locação/comodato)** or **surface-holder (direito de superfície)** — pays rent/fee to the vehicle, lives there, but holds **no equity, no owner vote, no withdrawal/severance mechanics**.
- This keeps the **ownership model** (individuals → severable shares) intact for Brazilian members, while the community stays open to non-owners.
- Governance: non-owner participants get a **voice, not a vote** on reserved matters (e.g. a residents' council); owner votes stay with owner members.
